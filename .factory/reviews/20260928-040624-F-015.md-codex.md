## Verdict
AGREE — no CRITICAL or HIGH findings.
The approach is viable, but the animation tests need explicit timing control, and the proposed transitions have two visual edge cases.

## Findings
- [C1] [MEDIUM] Tests to write first, `motion.spec.ts` — naming APIs does not establish a reliable timing test — assertions after `await click()` can miss the initial drop frame; eventual assertions can pass even when announcements arrive late. `announcer.ts` deliberately writes in `requestAnimationFrame`, so Approach §3’s “announcements stay synchronous” is inaccurate — capture status, live-region text and focus in a browser-side first-frame probe scheduled after the click handler; inspect animation timing/keyframes and pause/seek animations for intermediate geometry. Test natural completion separately, without polling beyond the 600 ms deadline.
- [C2] [MEDIUM] Approach §1, blanket square transitions — a hovered square can retain a transitioning transform after becoming occupied or disabled, temporarily putting its piece beneath the winning bar — use the existing diagonal sequence X: 0, 1, 3; before the final keyboard move, hover square 6, where O wins. Its hover transform transitions back to `none`, creating a stacking context that traps `.mark` below the sibling `.win-line`. [CSS transform stacking rules](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-transforms-1/#transform-rendering) support this consequence — scope press transitions to active empty squares so occupancy/disablement cancels the transform immediately; add this mixed mouse/keyboard regression.
- [C3] [MEDIUM] Approach §1, lift keyframes “on `top`” — animating only position makes the winning edge deepen immediately over the blanket 80 ms shadow transition, rather than during the delayed lift — design §4 specifies that the edge deepens with the lift; the referenced mockup explicitly includes `box-shadow: var(--shadow-tile)` in the lift’s starting keyframe — include shadow interpolation in the delayed 240 ms lift and verify the pre-lift and settled shadows.

## Missing
- Explicit timing checkpoints: drop start, lift delay, lift midpoint, settled state; compare numeric transforms with tolerances rather than serialized matrix strings.
- Reduced-motion preference changes during an active drop/lift, checking immediate cancellation and correct final appearance.
- A second valid move and a taken-square attempt before the previous drop finishes, covering class removal and interrupted animations.
- Real iOS Safari press validation: the fake EventTarget test proves listener registration, not visible `:active` feedback. Specify a held-touch check or manual device check.
- For taken-square E2E activation, reuse the existing forced activation helper: ordinary Playwright clicks treat `aria-disabled="true"` as disabled. [Playwright actionability](https://playwright.dev/docs/actionability)
